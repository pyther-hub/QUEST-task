FNO Darcy-flow surrogate, in your browser

−∇·(a∇u) = 1 on the unit square with u = 0 on the edge, on a 128×128 grid. Pick, draw or write a permeability a(x). The Fourier Neural Operator (ONNX, WebAssembly) predicts the pressure u(x), and a conjugate-gradient finite-volume solver computes it directly. Everything runs on this device.

Click and drag on the a(x) panel, then press Solve. The brush paints the two values in the training data, 0.1 and 1.

A JavaScript expression in x (left → right) and y (bottom → top), both in [0, 1], evaluated only in this browser. Math functions work without a prefix: sin, exp, hypot, PI, … With snap on, a true or > 0.5 result gives a = 1 and anything else gives a = 0.1. With snap off, values are clamped to [0.1, 1].

The model was trained on two-phase fields made from 5 smooth random blobs. Shapes outside that family (thin lines, checkerboards, smooth values between 0.1 and 1) are out of distribution, and the difference panel shows how much that costs. Timings are for one sample on this device (single-threaded WebAssembly for the FNO, plain JS for the solver).
